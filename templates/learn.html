LEARN & PROTECT

Know the signs. Stay in control.

Practical, independent steps can help you assess a job offer more safely.

₹

Upfront payment scams

A recruiter asks for a registration, training, equipment, or security fee before you can start.

ORIGINAL EXAMPLE

“Pay a refundable onboarding deposit today and your remote role is confirmed.”

Suspicious: upfront payment + pressure
⚑

Sensitive data requests

Someone asks for a payment PIN, OTP, banking login, or identity documents before you can verify the offer.

ORIGINAL EXAMPLE

“Send the verification code from your phone so payroll can activate your profile.”

Never share an OTP or payment PIN.
⌂

Work-from-home traps

Vague duties paired with unrealistic earnings, paid starter kits, or requests to install unknown apps.

GENUINE VS SUSPICIOUS

More credible: named duties, published salary range, official application process.

Warning signs: guaranteed income, fees, no verifiable company contact.

◎

Links & recruiter impersonation

A polished message or familiar-looking logo is not proof. Lookalike domains can imitate real organizations.

  • Type the official website address yourself.
  • Call a publicly listed company number.
  • Check the exact email domain, not only the sender name.

If you paid or shared sensitive information

  1. Contact your bank or payment provider immediately and ask about blocking or disputing the payment.
  2. Change affected passwords from a trusted device; revoke active sessions and enable multifactor security.
  3. If you shared an OTP, PIN, or card data, call your bank using its official number now.
  4. Save messages and receipts, report the incident to your local cybercrime or consumer-protection authority, and tell the platform.
  5. If identity documents were shared, contact the issuing authority for steps to reduce misuse risk.
